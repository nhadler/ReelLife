# Schedule

**Schedule** in the top bar plans what to write each day, across every
project that isn't archived. Open it from the top bar or Ctrl+K.

## What it plans

Every active cue still to write (To Write, In Progress) or to revise
(Rewrite, Revisions) that has an IN and OUT. Each cue counts toward its
project's composition deadline, the same one the daily pace uses: the
one chosen in the stats panel, else the next composition deadline.

Hard cues take longer, so the work is weighted by difficulty: Easy
counts once, Moderate twice and Hard three times its length, and
revisions half of that. A cue with no difficulty counts as Easy.

Change these under **Settings, Schedule** (admins and editors): the
factor for each difficulty and for revisions, what a cue with no
difficulty counts as, **Projects at Once** (below), **Most Work a Day**
(a cap, in minutes of Easy music; blank plans whatever the deadlines
need) and **Smallest Share** (the least a long cue is split into).

## How the days are filled

**Projects at Once** decides how projects share your days:

- **In parallel** (the default): every project is worked every day, each
  at its own pace: the least even amount that meets its own deadlines.
  A day's work is the projects' paces added up. If that's more than
  **Most Work a Day**, a project asking for less than an even share
  keeps all of it and the rest is shared by the others, so the cap comes
  out of the busiest projects.
- **One at a time**: earlier deadlines go first, and one pace covers
  everything: each day gets the least even amount that meets every
  deadline ahead. Once a deadline is met, the pace eases to what the
  later ones need.

Either way:

- Within a project's deadline, cues already in progress come first, then
  by priority (High, Medium, Low), project, reel and IN.
- Long cues split across days, each day showing its share of the music
  to write, e.g. **1:20 of 4:00**. No share is smaller than a quarter of
  a day.
- Your days off stop all work. A project's own days off (Project
  Settings, Deadlines) stop only that project's cues.
- Cues in projects with no composition deadline ahead come after
  everything else, at the last pace. Cues without an IN or OUT can't be
  planned and are listed under the calendar.

## When things change

The plan starts again from today every time you open it. Finish a cue
early and the next ones move up; leave one unfinished and it simply
moves into the coming days with the rest. Nothing is ever marked late.

## Log Written

To tell the schedule how far you got, use **Log Written…** beside a cue
in today's or a past day's list (or in the cue page's ⋯ menu, or the
grid's right-click menu): the day, and how much music you wrote, as
m:ss or minutes. It starts filled in with what was planned. The days
ahead then plan only what's left, though at least one Smallest Share
stays until you mark the cue composed. Logging the same cue and day
again replaces it; **Remove** takes it back, and so does **Undo**.

Only logged work counts: a planned day you didn't log just leaves the
work in the queue. Writing and revising are logged apart, so a cue sent
back for revisions starts its revision work from nothing.

Each day's plan is kept from the first time anyone looks (or
overnight), so a past day's list shows what was planned beside what you
logged, faded on the calendar.

The line above the calendar starts **On track** when every deadline is
met; with a **Most Work a Day** too low, it says which deadline slips
instead.

## Schedule On

To work on a cue on a particular day, pin it: **Schedule On…** in the
cue page's ⋯ menu, the grid's right-click menu, or beside the cue in a
day's list on the Schedule. Pick the day; the rest of the plan flows
around it, and a pin marks it on the calendar. **Unpin** in the same
dialog lets it flow again. Pinning is one change, so **Undo** and
Activity take it back. Once its day has passed, a pinned cue that isn't
finished rejoins the flow.

## In your calendar app

**Calendar Feed** in the profile menu makes a link your calendar app can
subscribe to: one all-day event per day with work, listing its cues,
the last 30 days as planned and the next 90. The link is shown once;
copy it, or click **Subscribe in Calendar**. Anyone with the link can
read your schedule, so **Revoke Link** if it gets out, and **Make a New
Link** to replace it. **Download .ics** gives a one-off copy instead.

Calendar apps on your own devices reach ReelLife wherever you can.
Google Calendar and iCloud's server-side refresh fetch the feed from
their own servers, so they only work once ReelLife is reachable from
the internet.

## Month and week

Both show a calendar, the week as a single row. Each project has its own
colour, down the edge of its cues. Click anywhere in a day to list its
work on the right: each cue with its title, status, project, difficulty
and that day's share of its music. Today is chosen when you arrive. In
the month, a busy day shows three cues and how many more. Days off are
hatched.

**Projects**, on the right, works like a calendar app's list of
calendars: tick the projects to show. The arrows, **Today**, and
**Month** and **Week** keep your choice.

Cues without an IN or OUT, and cues with nothing to plan toward, are
listed above the calendar.

## On the Overview

The Overview, ReelLife's home page, shows the next seven days of the
schedule. Click a day to open the Schedule on that week with the day
chosen.
